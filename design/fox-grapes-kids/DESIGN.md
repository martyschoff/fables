## 2. Centering thought and theological frame
Martin’s centering thought, verbatim:

> This fable warns against the sins of envy, rationalization, and a lack of contentment. It provides a vivid backdrop for teaching the fruit of the Spirit, specifically self-control (Galatians 5:22-23), and the wisdom of contentment (Philippians 4:11-12) instead of bitterly despising what we cannot have.

- Envy becomes wanting another creature’s access; rationalization becomes calling unreachable grapes sour without tasting them.
- Hunger, disappointment, and wanting something good are not themselves sins. The problem is turning disappointment into dishonest contempt.
- Self-control is fruit of the Holy Spirit; contentment is learned dependence on God, not a reward for suppressing feelings.
- Fern’s added reconsideration illustrates repentance and growth. God’s care does not depend on her behaving perfectly or obtaining grapes.
## 3. Audience and American-centric setting notes
- Default: ages 4–7, adult read-aloud; roughly 350 words of story and moral, plus Scripture and front/back matter.
- Setting: a fictional family vineyard in New York’s Finger Lakes region, late summer; no specific business or town.
- American visual cues: red equipment barn, split-rail fence, gravel farm lane, apple basket, distant blue hills.
- Keep the animal fable: Fern is a female red fox; an unnamed robin provides a quiet visual counterpart.
- Use familiar US English and concrete actions. Keep the emotional tone gentle, with humor in the jumps and room for disappointment.
- No human lecturer, punishment scene, denominational branding, or suggestion that poverty and hunger reflect moral failure.
## 4. Kid-adapted story text
**NEW retelling inspired by Riley’s source excerpt above; not verbatim Phaedrus, Riley, or Smart.**
Preserve hunger → repeated attempts → unreachable grapes → sour-grapes excuse. The pause, prayer, and reconsideration are new adaptation material. Proposed prose by printed page:
- **p4:** Along a farm lane, Fern the fox sniffed the warm summer air.
- **p5:** Sweet grapes hung from a vine overhead. Fern’s tummy rumbled.
- **p6:** A robin perched beside a bunch. Fern wished she could reach it, too.
- **p7:** She crouched low, swished her tail, and sprang. Almost!
- **p8:** She tried from the left. She tried from the right.
- **p9:** Up went her paws. Down came her paws. The grapes stayed high.
- **p10:** Fern backed up for one enormous jump.
- **p11:** Whoosh! Her nose brushed a leaf. Plop! She landed in the grass.
- **p12:** Fern sat down, puffing. She was tired, hungry, and disappointed.
- **p13:** Above her, the grapes glowed purple in the sun.
- **p14:** “They must be sour,” she grumbled. “I wouldn’t want them anyway.”
- **p15:** She turned away with her nose in the air.
- **p16:** But after a few steps, Fern stopped.
- **p17:** She had not tasted even one grape. How could she know?
- **p18:** “I wanted them,” she admitted. “I’m upset because I can’t reach them.”
- **p19:** She let out a long, slow breath.
- **p20:** “God, please help me tell the truth and be thankful.”
- **p21:** The grapes still hung high. Fern’s tummy still rumbled.
- **p22:** She could be disappointed without saying something unkind.
- **p23:** “You may be perfectly good grapes,” she said.
- **p24:** “I just can’t reach you today.”
- **p25:** Fern padded to the shady creek and took a cool drink.
- **p26:** Then she rested her tired paws beneath a willow.
- **p27:** Soon she would look for supper. For now, she could leave the grapes in peace.
- **p28:** When we cannot have something, calling it bad does not make us truthful.
- **p29:** With God’s help, we can practice self-control and learn contentment.

## 6. Art direction
- Warm watercolor and colored pencil, soft edges, expressive animal posture, lightly textured paper.
- Palette: russet fox, plum grapes, sage leaves, golden grass, creek blue, warm cream.
- Fern stays recognizably quadrupedal, with consistent proportions and no clothing; emotion reads through ears, tail, eyes, and posture.
- Establish a tall grape arbor at the vineyard edge; keep fruit height and Fern’s scale consistent throughout.
- Reserve uncluttered text areas; keep faces, text, and essential actions clear of the gutter.
- Avoid sinister grapes, mocking birds, injury humor, frightening teeth, magical divine light, and a final reward bunch. Grapes must never appear proven sour.
